Why it matters

Asylum interviews are unlike most legal proceedings. The applicant is expected to recount some of the most difficult experiences of their life, under pressure, to a stranger. Their credibility will be judged on consistency, demeanor, and how their story aligns with country conditions the officer already knows.

Most applicants have never been in that room before. A mock interview conducted by someone who has run hundreds of them from the other side is the closest preparation available.

What the session covers

  • Opening and background questions

    Name, address, travel history. These questions seem easy but set the tone and establish the record.

  • Basis for protection

    Direct and follow-up questions on the claimed basis: persecution, fear of return, nexus to a protected ground.

  • Credibility probes

    The kind of follow-up questions a skeptical officer actually asks: inconsistencies, omissions, timing.

  • Demeanor and delivery coaching

    Guidance on pacing, eye contact, how to handle confusion or distress, and when to pause.

Available for

  • Affirmative asylum interviews (USCIS Asylum Office)
  • Defensive asylum hearings (Immigration Court)
  • Refugee interviews
  • Credible fear and reasonable fear screenings
  • Humanitarian parole applications with interviews
